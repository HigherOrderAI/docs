HigherOrder carries three risk labels, each answering a different question. The **Assessment Tier** is set at intake and decides how deeply a vendor is reviewed. The **vendor Risk Band** is the synthesized 0–100 view of a vendor across all evidence. The **assessment risk score** — new in August 2026 — is a deterministic 0–100 score computed from the vendor's questionnaire answers on a single assessment. All three use *higher means more risk*.

## The vendor Risk Band — six bands, 0–100

Every vendor-level risk surface — vendor inventory row, vendor profile risk overview, per-document analysis (SOC 2, ISO 27001, VAPT, Privacy Policy), contract gap risk — is expressed on the same 0–100 scale, mapped to one of six named bands. The band is the authoritative label; the numeric score is shown alongside it.

| Band | Score range | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Minimal** | 0–20 | Little to no material risk |
| **Low** | 21–40 | Acceptable with standard controls |
| **Moderate** | 41–60 | Warrants attention; may require mitigations |
| **High** | 61–75 | Significant exposure; controls required |
| **Very High** | 76–90 | Immediate attention warranted |
| **Critical** | 91–100 | Do not proceed without mitigations or executive sign-off |

The score is composed by the platform from the evidence available for the vendor:

- **Document analyses.** Each analyzer (SOC 2, ISO 27001, VAPT, Privacy Policy, Questionnaire, Contract) produces a category score. Missing or expired evidence contributes to a higher score.
- **Threat model.** The entity-level threat assessment built from all of the vendor's documents (an uploaded architecture diagram enriches it but is not required).
- **Contract gaps.** Where a vendor commitment is weaker than what you have promised your own customers (see [Customer Golden Standards](/customer-risk/golden-standards)).
- **Ecosystem signals.** From connected IdP / SSPM (Okta, Google Workspace, Entra ID) — undisclosed OAuth grants, undisclosed new SKUs. See Drift Center.

The overall band is a synthesis across these inputs, weighted by the assessment tier. The synthesis is regenerated when new evidence lands, when a document is reprocessed, or on explicit request.

## The assessment risk score — deterministic, answer-driven

Each vendor assessment carries its own 0–100 risk score computed **entirely from the vendor's answers** — no model call, no evidence weighting. The same answers always produce the same score, and the methodology replicates the scoring model many risk teams already run in their incumbent systems, so scores are comparable during a migration.

### How the score is built

<Steps>
  <Step title="Options carry scores">
    Each answer option on a yes/no, single-select, or multi-select question carries a numeric score, set on the questionnaire template. Each question declares a scoring range and a direction — whether a higher option value means more risk or less.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Questions normalize and weight">
    An answer is normalized within its question's range, then weighted. Question weights are set on the template's Scoring page, which is the single owner of the weights the score uses.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sections roll up to 0–100">
    Weighted question scores average within each sub-category; sub-category weights combine into a 0–100 score per top-level section. Sections average into the questionnaire score, and — for a merged assessment — questionnaire scores average into the assessment score.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The score maps to a rating">
    The 0–100 score maps to one of four ratings: **Very Low / Low / Medium / High**. Band ranges are configured per questionnaire template and must tile the full 0–100 range. The default scale is 0–15 Very Low, 16–30 Low, 31–45 Medium, 46+ High.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
The assessment rating vocabulary (**Very Low → High**, four values, per-template band ranges) is deliberately distinct from the six-band vendor scale above. Both run 0–100 with higher meaning more risk, but an assessment's "High" is defined by that questionnaire's configured bands, not the global vendor tiling.
</Note>

### When it's computed

- **During the assessment** the score is computed live, on demand, and marked non-final. It is suppressed until answered questions cover at least 40% of the questionnaire's scorable weight — a thin early score is worse than none. Per-domain scores are the exception: each domain's chip in the review workbench rail appears from that domain's first scored answer, since a domain score only claims to describe its own domain.
- **At completion** the score and per-section breakdown are stamped onto the assessment and never recomputed, so the screen and the exported report can never disagree.

**Coverage** is reported alongside the score: the share of scorable question *weight* that has been answered — so an unanswered Critical question moves coverage more than an unanswered Low one.

### What enters the score

Only yes/no, single-select, and multi-select questions with fully scored options participate. Everything else is **excluded from the calculation entirely — never counted as zero risk**: text questions, unanswered questions, answers marked not-applicable, questions withdrawn from scope, and any answer that doesn't match a configured option.

### Where you'll see it

- **Review workbench** — a live score chip in the domain rail while the review is in flight, plus a per-domain score chip on each domain that moves as the vendor answers and is stamped final at completion
- **Assessment and engagement views** — the rating badge on each questionnaire, with a per-section **Score Breakdown** panel after completion
- **Template Scoring page** — where admins set option scores, ranges, directions, weights, and band ranges, with a preview simulator that shows the best-case and worst-case score for the configuration

### Limitations

- Text answers never contribute to the score; a questionnaire of mostly text questions will score on its scored minority or be suppressed as not scorable.
- Attestation mode changes what evidence is required for verification — it does not change the score. Evidence quality does not enter the assessment risk score at all; it is an answers-only number.
- When an assessment is launched on a subset of a questionnaire's categories, the score normalizes over the subset while the bands were calibrated for the full questionnaire — the breakdown flags this.
- Completed assessments are never re-scored, even if the template's scoring configuration changes later.

## Assessment Tier — the intake-only label

Assessment Tier is set during Vendor Intake based on the requester's declared exposure — data types (health, financial, source code, PII), scale of rollout, AI usage, integrations. It routes the request to the right review depth:

| Tier | Review path | SLA |
|---|---|---|
| **Low** | Automated brief, no analyst review | Immediate |
| **Medium** | Analyst review with targeted output package | 2 weeks |
| **High** | Full assessment with document collection, legal + security sync | 4 weeks |
| **Critical** | Executive sign-off required, full dossier, security leadership flagged | Same-day escalation |

Assessment Tier is **declared risk**, not validated risk. It is displayed on the intake detail during review. Once the vendor is linked to a formal assessment and the platform has processed evidence, the **validated Risk Band** appears alongside it. Where they disagree, the Risk Band is authoritative.

## Risk Coverage

Risk Coverage is the completeness of the evidence base behind the vendor-level score, expressed as points (out of the maximum coverage points achievable for a given assessment). A vendor with 45 / 60 coverage points has less confident scoring than one with 58 / 60, even if both land in the same band. Coverage is shown alongside the band on the vendor profile.

## Where you'll see this

- **Vendor Inventory** — the risk grade column shows band + score (e.g., `High · 65`) rather than a letter grade
- **Vendor Profile** — Validated Risk section shows the band first, then coverage
- **Per-document analysis** — each document's contribution is scored on the same scale
- **Assessments** — each assessment carries its own answer-driven score and four-value rating (see above)
- **Reports** — the downloadable vendor risk report leads with the band and coverage
- **Contract Coverage** — gap risk uses the same band scale
- **Findings** — severity of an individual finding is Critical / High / Medium / Low (four levels) — a per-finding attribute distinct from both scales above
